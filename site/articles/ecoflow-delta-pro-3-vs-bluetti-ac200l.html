EcoFlow DELTA Pro 3 vs Bluetti AC200L

Direct answer: The EcoFlow DELTA Pro 3 stores roughly twice the energy of the Bluetti AC200L and currently costs less per watt-hour: $0.68/Wh against $0.78/Wh at the cheapest in-stock offer we track ($2,799.00 and $1,599.00 respectively). The AC200L is the smaller cheque; the DELTA Pro 3 is the cheaper energy. Which matters depends on whether you are buying capacity or buying an entry price.

By Brandon Hall · Prices on this page refreshed when the site was last built; price record runs from 2026-08-13 to 2026-08-25.

These two land in the same shopping list surprisingly often, and they are not really the same class of machine. One is a 4 kWh home-backup-scale unit; the other is a 2 kWh portable. Comparing them is still useful, because the question most people are actually asking is how much battery should I buy at this price point.

Helios has not physically tested any product on this page. Our authority is price data, published specifications and our own scrape history — that is a narrower claim than a review site makes, and it is the one we can actually support.

Reading the two tables together

The comparison that survives scrutiny is cost per watt-hour on the main unit at each retailer, which is the figure in the $/Wh column above. Kit variants carry a bundle badge and no rating, because dividing a bundle price by the battery's capacity credits the panels and cables to the battery and produces a number that is wrong by construction.

Capacity is the honest headline difference. The DELTA Pro 3 stores roughly twice what the AC200L does, and the spec table above records a substantially higher continuous output for it, so it can hold up continuous loads the AC200L cannot. Whether it will start a load the AC200L cannot is a different question and we are not answering it: starting surge is a separate rating, our catalog does not store it, and inferring it from continuous output would be a guess dressed as a spec. If your constraint is the size of the cheque rather than the size of the load, that extra capability is money spent on headroom you may never use.

What we cannot compare

This is the part most head-to-heads skip, so here it is explicitly. We cannot tell you:

  • Real runtime under a real load. We have not run either unit. Rated capacity is a nameplate figure; what you get depends on the load, the temperature and the inverter's efficiency at that draw.
  • Cycle life in practice. Both are LiFePO4 and both manufacturers publish cycle ratings. We have not verified either, and we have not owned either long enough to have an opinion.
  • Noise, app quality, firmware, or support. No data. Not measured, not inferred.
  • Warranty handling. We have never filed a claim with either company.

Retailer coverage is also uneven between these two, and the tables above show it: a product carried by one tracked retailer has no cross-retailer price check at all, so its price is a single observation rather than a corroborated one.
