LiFePO4 vs NCM, in plain English

Direct answer: LiFePO4 (lithium iron phosphate) trades energy density for cycle life and thermal stability; NCM (nickel-cobalt-manganese) trades the opposite way, packing more watt-hours into less weight. For home backup and most portable power stations the industry has settled on LiFePO4, and our catalog reflects that: of the products where a chemistry is recorded, 21 are LiFePO4 and 1 is NCM. Live examples of each are below, with the caveat that we read chemistry off spec sheets rather than cells.

By Brandon Hall · Prices on this page refreshed when the site was last built; price record runs from 2026-08-13 to 2026-08-22.

The trade, in one paragraph

Both are lithium-ion. The difference is what the cathode is made of, and that choice sets everything else. LiFePO4 — lithium iron phosphate, sometimes written LFP — uses iron and phosphate: cheap, abundant, chemically stable, and comparatively heavy for the energy it stores. NCM — nickel, cobalt, manganese, sometimes NMC — packs more energy into less mass and volume, which is why it dominated the first generation of portable power stations and why it remains common in electric vehicles where range and pack density set the design, particularly in Europe and in premium long-range models. It is no longer the majority chemistry in EVs globally, though: the IEA puts LFP at more than half of all EV batteries deployed worldwide in 2025, overtaking the nickel-based chemistries.

  • Electric vehicle batteries — Global EV Outlook 2026 — International Energy Agency, 2026-05-20. LFP accounted for over 55% of EV batteries deployed globally in 2025, up from nearly 50% in 2024, passing the nickel-based chemistries. We have not independently verified these figures; we are citing them.

What each one is better at

  • Cycle life favours LiFePO4, usually by a wide margin. Manufacturers typically rate LFP packs for several thousand cycles to 80% capacity, and NCM packs for something in the 800 to 2,000 range. Those are published ratings, not our measurements.
  • Energy density favours NCM. For the same watt-hours, an NCM pack is lighter and smaller.
  • Thermal behaviour favours LiFePO4, which is the main reason it has taken over stationary storage: it is the more forgiving chemistry when something goes wrong.
  • Cost per watt-hour currently favours LiFePO4 on the products we track, which is visible directly in the tables below rather than asserted here.

For a battery that lives in a garage and cycles daily for a decade, the density penalty costs you floor space and the cycle life pays you back every day. For something you carry, the trade is genuinely closer.

Helios has not physically tested any product on this page. Our authority is price data, published specifications and our own scrape history — that is a narrower claim than a review site makes, and it is the one we can actually support.
