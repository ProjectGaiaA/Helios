What $/Wh tells you, and what it hides

Direct answer: Cost per watt-hour is a purchase price divided by a published capacity, and it is only meaningful when both halves describe the same standalone thing. It tells you which battery stores energy most cheaply. It hides cycle life, usable depth of discharge, inverter capability, warranty, and everything a kit bundles in. Helios computes it for one variant at a time and withholds it entirely for bundles and multi-packs, because a kit price over a battery's capacity produces a precise-looking number that is wrong by construction. This page is the full rule set.

By Brandon Hall · Prices on this page refreshed when the site was last built; price record runs from 2026-08-13 to 2026-09-02.

Cost per watt-hour is the most useful single number in battery shopping and the easiest one to compute wrongly. This page is both an explainer and our public specification: the rules below are the rules the site enforces in code, not aspirations.

What it is

A retailer's price for one standalone product, divided by that product's published capacity in watt-hours. Nothing else. It answers exactly one question — which battery stores energy most cheaply — and answers it well.

The four rules we enforce

  1. One variant at a time. The price and the rating in any row always describe the same variant. Pairing a discounted bundle's price with a bare unit's rating is a defect we have shipped once and now test against.
  2. Bundles get no rating. If the listing sells a kit, a multi-pack or a battery-plus-panels bundle, we show the price and withhold the $/Wh. A kit price over a battery's capacity is wrong by construction, and the wrongness scales with how good the kit is.
  3. Unknown capacity withholds. Capacity is used only where a tracked listing states it, backed by a verbatim quote from that listing. Where two retailers disagree about a capacity, we publish no rating for that product at any retailer.
  4. Stale and disputed numbers come off the page. Prices older than our staleness threshold are withheld rather than shown with a caveat, and any figure our audit finds disagreeing with its own source is quarantined until it verifies clean.

The rule, visible

Here is the discipline working on a real product. The single-battery variant carries a rating; the multi-battery variants of the same product carry prices and a bundle badge, and no rating at all — because we do not know from the listing alone that a “2 Batteries” variant is exactly twice the capacity.

What it hides

  • Cycle life. Two batteries at the same $/Wh are not the same purchase if one lasts three times as long. Divide by rated cycles and the ranking can invert.
  • Usable depth of discharge. Nameplate capacity is not always drawable capacity.
  • Everything that is not the battery. Inverter capability, outlets, solar input, app, warranty, and whether the thing needs an electrician.
  • Total installed cost. Especially for rack batteries, where the battery is a component of a system you have to finish buying.
  • Shipping and tax. Not in any price on this site.

Use $/Wh to shortlist and to catch a bad price. Do not use it to pick a winner on its own.

Why we would rather show nothing

A missing number is a gap a reader can see and work around. A wrong number looks exactly like a right one. Every withheld rating on this site prints the rule that withheld it, so you can judge whether the gap matters to you rather than wondering whether we simply failed to look.
