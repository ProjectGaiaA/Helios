When do power stations actually go on sale?

Direct answer: Published deal coverage puts the big power-station discounts on the US retail-holiday calendar — Earth Day, Memorial Day, Amazon's Prime Day window, and the Fourth of July — with 48- and 72-hour manufacturer flash sales running inside those windows. That is the pattern to plan around, and it is sourced below to dated reporting rather than to us. Our own record currently spans 9 days of scrapes (2026-08-13 to 2026-08-21), which is nowhere near enough to call a cadence ourselves. What we can offer is the measurement going forward, twice a day, with every observation dated.

The pattern, from published coverage

Power-station pricing is not a smooth market. It runs on manufacturer-led promotional windows anchored to the US retail calendar, with short flash sales inside them. The deal-coverage record is the best public evidence of that cadence, so here it is, dated and linked rather than summarised from memory.

  • Electrified Weekly — Earth Day power station sales from EcoFlow + Bluetti, 72-hour Anker SOLIX flash sale — 9to5Toys, 2026-04-11. Earth Day promotions from two manufacturers running alongside a 72-hour Anker SOLIX weekend flash sale.
  • Bluetti exclusive Memorial Day power station lows, EcoFlow 48-hour flash sale — Electrek, 2026-05-25. A 48-hour EcoFlow flash sale running through May 26, nested inside a longer seasonal sale.
  • EcoFlow Early Prime Day power station deals up to 62% off — Electrek, 2026-06-08. A 48-hour flash sale inside an 'early Prime Day' window, weeks ahead of Amazon's own event.
  • July 4th Green Deals: Bluetti Summer Power Sale, EcoFlow flash sale — Electrek, 2026-07-03. Independence Day promotions, with one manufacturer running a season-long sale in place of a holiday event.

4 dated sources spanning 2026-04-11 to 2026-07-03 (83 days). The gaps between consecutive sources run from 14 to 44 days — measured from the dates above, not characterised from memory.

Read together, those reports describe anchors rather than a cadence. The promotional windows they cover sit on the US retail calendar — Earth Day, Memorial Day, the Prime Day period and the Fourth of July — with 48- to 72-hour flash sales used as the sharpest price point inside each window, and with off-cycle events (an “early Prime Day” weeks ahead of Amazon's own) sitting between them. The intervals between the dated reports above are uneven, as the line under them shows, so what this evidence supports is several promotional windows inside any given quarter, not a clock you can set. The flash sales are where the genuine lows appear, and they are short enough that you have to already be watching.

Note what those citations are and are not. They are evidence that promotional windows exist and roughly when. They are not evidence that any particular product hits its lowest price in them, and we have not verified the individual prices quoted in that coverage.

How to actually catch a flash sale

  • Decide your target price before the window opens. A 48-hour sale is designed to prevent deliberation.
  • Watch the manufacturer's own store as well as the retailers — several of the sales in the coverage above were manufacturer-direct.
  • Check the compare-at price against a real history, not against the retailer's own struck-through figure.
  • Remember that the sharpest discounts in that coverage attach to specific SKUs, not to whole ranges.

Price alerts: planned, not built
