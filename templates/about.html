About Helios

What this is

Helios is a price tracker for solar and home-energy equipment: portable power stations, server-rack and wall-mount batteries, deep-cycle batteries, solar panels, inverters and balance-of-system parts. It reads what retailers publish, records it, and shows the numbers side by side with the date they were read.

It currently tracks {{ facts.product_count }} products across {{ facts.retailer_count }} retailers ({% for name in facts.retailer_names %}{{ name }}{% if not loop.last %}, {% endif %}{% endfor %}), covering {{ facts.rendered_pairs }} product-retailer pairs. That is the entire catalog, not a selection from a larger one — if a product is tracked, it is on the site, including the ones where the comparison turns out to be unflattering or where no comparison is possible at all.

Helios is an independent project run by one person. It is not affiliated with any manufacturer or retailer, and no retailer has any input into what is tracked or how it is ranked.

Where the numbers come from

Prices are read directly from each retailer's own product data endpoints — the same structured feed their storefront uses — rather than by scraping rendered pages. Availability is read from a separate per-variant endpoint, because the price feed does not carry it and guessing stock is how a tracker ends up confidently wrong.

Collection is deliberately slow and identifiable. Helios checks robots.txt before it fetches, identifies itself with a real contact address in its user agent, and waits between five and fifteen seconds between requests. It reads catalog data only: it never adds to a cart, never starts a checkout, and never places an order. Both of the retailers Helios started with state in their own robots.txt that checkout is for humans, and that is treated here as a standing rule rather than a preference.

How often prices are checked

Prices are collected on a schedule, twice daily (morning and evening UTC), with occasional extra manual runs. A claimed cadence is the easiest lie on the site to tell and the hardest for a reader to check, so every price also displays its own age — if the schedule ever silently stops, the ages on the page say so before we do.

Withhold when unknown

This is the rule the whole site is built around: when Helios does not know something, it shows nothing rather than an estimate. A missing number is a gap the reader can see and work around. A wrong number looks exactly like a right one.

In practice:

  • Cost per watt-hour is only computed for standalone units. Most listings sell kits — a battery plus panels, or a pack of several batteries — under one price. Dividing a kit's price by one component's capacity produces a precise-looking number that is wrong by construction. Those variants get their price shown and a "bundle" badge, and no $/Wh.
  • Capacity is only used when a retailer actually states it. Of {{ facts.product_count }} tracked products, {{ facts.capacity_known }} carry a capacity figure and {{ facts.capacity_withheld }} do not. Where a figure is recorded, it is backed by a verbatim quote extracted from the merchant's own listing text — {{ facts.capacity_quoted }} of the {{ facts.capacity_known }} have that evidence stored and re-checked against the live listing on later audits.
  • Conflicting sources withhold. Where two retailers state capacities for the same battery that differ by an order of magnitude, the honest state is "unknown", so no $/Wh is published for that product at any retailer — even though picking one of the two figures would have filled the column in.
  • Panels are rated in watts, not watt-hours, so their capacity field is empty by nature. Their $/W comes from the nameplate output recorded in the catalog, which does not yet carry the same verbatim-quote evidence that capacity does. That weaker provenance is marked on the page wherever a $/W is shown.

Checking the site against itself

A tracker's real failure mode is not a bad match — it is that every check is self-referential, so an error introduced at one step is invisible at the next. Helios runs an audit that samples product-retailer-variant combinations and compares them along two independent hops: what the built page actually displays against the stored price row, and that stored row against the retailer's live data.

The two hops are treated differently on purpose. A page disagreeing with its own stored data is a defect: that variant is quarantined and withheld from the site until it verifies clean. A stored row disagreeing with the live retailer is normal — prices move between checks — so it prompts a re-scrape and never removes anything.

To make this checkable rather than a claim, every displayed number carries machine-readable provenance in the HTML: the variant id, the retailer's SKU, the price tier, and the timestamp it was scraped. View the source of any comparison table on this site and each number can be traced to the exact record it came from.

What Helios does not do

  • No hands-on testing, teardowns, or product reviews. Helios compares published prices and published specifications. Nothing here is an opinion about whether a product is good.
  • No paid placement. Rankings are computed from price data; there is no field in the system a retailer could pay to change.
  • No shipping, tax, installation, or rebate modelling. The prices are the retailers' listed prices, which is often not what you finally pay.
  • No checkout, cart, or order automation, ever.
  • No claims about how many people use this site, how long it has run, or who endorses it.
